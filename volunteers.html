Teaching Roles

👨‍🏫

Lead Teacher

Most Needed

Role: Design and deliver complete courses in your area of expertise. Lead class sessions, create assignments, and assess student progress.

Commitment: 4-6 hours per week for one term (8-16 weeks depending on term)

Requirements:

  • ✓ Expertise in subject area (English, Math, Science, Arts, etc.)
  • ✓ Teaching or tutoring experience preferred but not required
  • ✓ Strong communication skills
  • ✓ Reliable internet and computer access
  • ✓ Available for scheduled class times

Subjects Needed: English (all levels), Math, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Computer Science, Engineering, Social Sciences

🎓

Co-Teacher / Teaching Assistant

Great for Beginners

Role: Support a lead teacher by helping with lessons, answering student questions, providing breakout room support, and grading assignments.

Commitment: 2-4 hours per week for one term

Requirements:

  • ✓ Knowledge in relevant subject area
  • ✓ Enthusiasm for supporting student learning
  • ✓ Reliable attendance for class sessions
  • ✓ No prior teaching experience required

Perfect for: First-time volunteers, college students, or anyone wanting to test the waters before lead teaching

🌍

Afghan Co-Teacher

Special Role

Role: Partner with international teachers to provide cultural context, language support (Dari/Pashto), and native understanding of Afghan student needs.

Commitment: 3-5 hours per week for one term

Requirements:

  • ✓ Afghan background or deep cultural knowledge
  • ✓ Fluent in English and Dari/Pashto
  • ✓ Understanding of Afghan student context
  • ✓ Teaching experience helpful but not required

Impact: Many of our Afghan co-teachers are former AESOP students! This role helps you develop teaching skills for potential future careers.

🎨

Specialized Course Creator

Creative Freedom

Role: Design and teach unique courses based on your passion and expertise. Past examples: Drawing, Latin, Meditation, Public Speaking, Debate.

Commitment: Flexible - design your own course structure

Requirements:

  • ✓ Unique skill or expertise to share
  • ✓ Creativity in curriculum design
  • ✓ Passion for your subject
  • ✓ Ability to make content accessible to students

Examples: Art, Music, Creative Writing, Journalism, Environmental Science, Health & Wellness, Business Skills, Technology

Volunteer Onboarding Process

Step 1

Submit Application

Tell us about your skills, interests, availability, and what role you're interested in. Share your passion for education and social impact.

Step 2

Interview & Matching

We'll have a conversation to learn more about you and find the best fit for your skills and schedule. You'll learn more about AESOP and specific role expectations.

Step 3

Training & Orientation

Complete systems training, teaching methods training (for teachers), and well-being/boundary training. Meet other volunteers and learn AESOP's tools and practices.

Step 4

Buddy System Setup

Get paired with an experienced volunteer who can help you navigate the ins and outs of AESOP and offer ongoing support.

Step 5

Start Volunteering!

Begin your role, whether teaching classes, supporting students, or helping with administration. Make your impact!

Ongoing

Support & Growth

Receive ongoing support, participate in volunteer community meetings, and have opportunities to expand your role or try new things.

Volunteer FAQs

Do I need teaching experience?

Not necessarily! While teaching experience is helpful for lead teacher roles, we welcome volunteers of all experience levels. Co-teachers and teaching assistants don't need prior experience, and we provide training for all volunteers.

How much time do I need to commit?

This varies by role. Teaching roles typically require 2-6 hours per week (including class time and prep). Administrative roles range from 2-8 hours per week. You choose your commitment level.

Can I volunteer if I'm not a native English speaker?

Yes! While lead English teachers should be fluent in English, many roles are perfect for non-native speakers. Afghan volunteers who speak Dari/Pashto are especially valuable as co-teachers and in administrative roles.

What if I can only volunteer for one term?

That's perfectly fine! We appreciate any time you can give. Many volunteers start with one term and return for more, but there's no long-term commitment required.

Is this volunteer work remote?

Yes, 100% remote! All AESOP operations are online. You can volunteer from anywhere in the world with reliable internet access.

Can college students volunteer?

Absolutely! College students make excellent volunteers, especially as co-teachers, teaching assistants, or in administrative roles. This is great experience for future careers in education, international development, or social impact.

What if I want to create my own course?

We love this! AESOP supports volunteers in creating courses that match their passion and expertise. From Drawing to Latin to Meditation - if you have knowledge to share and can make it accessible, we'll work with you to develop it.

Will I receive a certificate or letter of recommendation?

Yes! We provide volunteer certificates and can write letters of recommendation for volunteers who complete their commitments successfully.

How do you ensure volunteer safety and well-being?

All volunteers complete well-being and boundary training. We have a buddy system for support, regular check-ins, and clear guidelines for maintaining healthy boundaries while doing emotionally engaged work.
